Question 1: Specific Heat Capacity & Thermal Energy Transfer
Calculate the amount of heat energy (in Joules) required to raise the temperature of 2.5 kg of pure water from 25°C to 85°C. (Specific heat capacity of water, c = 4,186 J/(kg·°C)).
Solution & Step-by-Step Working:
Given:
  • Mass of water, m = 2.5 kg
  • Initial temperature, T₁ = 25°C
  • Final temperature, T₂ = 85°C
  • Temperature change, ΔT = T₂ - T₁ = 85 - 25 = 60°C
  • Specific heat capacity, c = 4,186 J/(kg·°C)
Formula: Q = m × c × ΔT
Q = 2.5 kg × 4,186 J/(kg·°C) × 60°C Q = 2.5 × 251,160 Q = 627,900 Joules = 627.9 kJ
✓ Final Answer: Heat energy required Q = 627,900 J (627.9 kJ)
Question 2: First Law of Thermodynamics
A gas system in a piston absorbs 1,450 J of heat energy from its surroundings. During the expansion, the gas does 820 J of mechanical work on the piston. Calculate the change in internal energy (ΔU) of the system.
Solution & Step-by-Step Working:
According to the First Law of Thermodynamics:
ΔU = Q - W where: Q = Heat absorbed by system = +1,450 J W = Work done by system = +820 J ΔU = 1,450 J - 820 J = +630 Joules
✓ Final Answer: Internal energy increase ΔU = +630 J
Question 3: Ideal Gas Law Application
An ideal gas sample occupies a volume of 0.05 m³ at a pressure of 101.3 kPa and a temperature of 300 K. Find the number of moles (n) of gas present. (Universal gas constant R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)).
Solution:
Using the Ideal Gas Equation: P × V = n × R × T
n = (P × V) / (R × T) n = (101,300 Pa × 0.05 m³) / (8.314 × 300 K) n = 5,065 / 2,494.2 ≈ 2.03 moles
✓ Final Answer: Quantity of gas n ≈ 2.03 moles
Question 4: Modes of Heat Transfer Analysis
Differentiate briefly between Conduction, Convection, and Radiation with one daily life example for each.
Comparative Analysis:
  1. Conduction: Transfer of heat through direct atomic collisions without bulk movement of matter.
    Example: A metal spoon heating up in a hot cup of tea.
  2. Convection: Transfer of heat by the actual physical movement of heated fluid molecules (liquid or gas).
    Example: Boiling water in a pot where warm water rises and cold water sinks.
  3. Radiation: Transfer of thermal energy via electromagnetic waves through vacuum or medium.
    Example: Warming of the Earth by solar infrared radiation from the Sun.
✓ Concept Verified: Vacuum allows only Radiation.
